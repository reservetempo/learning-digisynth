Further reading

Everything worth reading twice, in one place. Each lesson links to the few items that matter to it; this page is the whole library, grouped by subject. Nothing here is behind a paywall and nothing needs an account.

If you only ever follow one link from this site, make it Synth Secrets. It is 63 articles long and it will teach you more about what this instrument is for than any amount of C++ will.

Understanding synthesizers

  • Synth Secrets — Gordon Reid, Sound On Sound 63 monthly articles, 1999–2004, and still the best explanation of synthesis in existence. Starts at "what is a sound" and ends at synthesizing a snare drum. Read one a week.
  • Learning Synths — Ableton Interactive, in the browser, no installation. An hour here will make every word in Part D and Part H of this site land properly. Start here if the vocabulary is the problem.

Real-time audio programming

  • Real-time audio programming 101: time waits for nothing — Ross Bencina The single most important article for understanding why the audio thread has such strange rules. Twenty minutes, and everything in Part E will make sense afterwards. (The site is http-only — your browser may warn about that.)
  • Audio Developer Conference (ADC) — YouTube The annual conference for exactly this kind of work. Talks on real-time safety, SIMD, filter design, plugin architecture. Free, and the quality is unusually high.
  • The Audio Programmer — YouTube The friendliest on-ramp to JUCE and audio C++. Tutorial-shaped rather than conference-shaped; good when you want to be shown rather than told.

Digital signal processing

  • EarLevel Engineering — oscillators Nigel Redmon's series on wavetable oscillators is the clearest practical writing on the subject anywhere, and it is exactly what Part G builds.
  • Introduction to Digital Filters — Julius O. Smith III A complete free textbook from Stanford's CCRMA. Heavier going and worth it; when a lesson here says "the maths is a field of its own", this is the field.
  • Spectral Audio Signal Processing — Julius O. Smith III The companion volume, on the frequency domain. Background for Part G's FFT and spectral morphing lessons.
  • Cytomic technical papers — Andy Simper The papers behind the state-variable filter design this project uses. Short, dense, and the reference implementations are in them.
  • musicdsp.org A decades-old archive of code snippets and algorithms. Unpolished, sometimes wrong, occasionally exactly the thing you needed.
  • dsprelated.com Articles, forums and free books. Good for looking up a term you have just met and want three explanations of.

Learning C++

  • learncpp.com The best free structured C++ course. If any snippet on this site is opaque at the language level rather than the audio level, the answer is here.
  • cppreference.com The reference, not a tutorial. Terse and authoritative — the place to check exactly what a standard-library thing promises.
  • Compiler Explorer (godbolt.org) Paste C++ in, see the machine instructions the compiler emits, live. The fastest way to make Part C's SIMD lessons concrete: you can literally watch eight numbers get added by one instruction.

Concurrency and performance

  • Preshing on Programming — Jeff Preshing Lock-free programming and memory ordering, explained with diagrams instead of standardese. The prerequisite reading for lesson c6.
  • Intel Intrinsics Guide Searchable documentation for every SIMD instruction, including the _mm256_* functions Part C uses. Look one up when a snippet names it.

Tools and frameworks

  • JUCE tutorials — official The framework this instrument is built on. The audio and GUI tutorials cover most of Parts B and L.
  • JUCE — YouTube Official talks and walkthroughs, including the annual JUCE summit sessions.
  • An Introduction to Modern CMake — Henry Schreiner CMake explained as it should be written today rather than as it was written in 2010. Background for lesson b1.

Curiosities worth an evening

  • The Euclidean Algorithm Generates Traditional Musical Rhythms — Godfried Toussaint (PDF) A 2,300-year-old algorithm for finding common divisors turns out to generate the bell patterns of West Africa, the Cuban tresillo and the Turkish aksak. This is the paper behind Part K's sequencer.
  • Vital — Matt Tytel (source code) A modern spectral-warping wavetable synth, open source. This project copies no code from it, but its feature set is the target and its colour palette is reused. Worth reading to see what a finished version of this looks like.
